# ORB + VWAP Filter — 0DTE Backtest (2025 & 2026)

Historical replay of the live bot (`orb_vwap_filter.py`) on **real QQQ 0DTE option
1-minute prices**, reproduced by [`backtest_vwap_filter.py`](backtest_vwap_filter.py).

## No look-ahead

The live bot is a **1-minute poller**: every minute it reads the last *completed* bar
plus the current option `marketPrice`, then submits **market orders**. The backtest
honours that exactly:

- **Every decision is made on a completed bar's CLOSE**, and the resulting fill happens
  on the **NEXT bar's OPEN** — never on the same bar that produced the signal.
- **No intrabar high is used to "capture" a target price** the poller never saw. Profit
  exits trigger when the *observed close* crosses the threshold and fill at the next open.
- The **only** intrabar fill is the −50% **stop**, because the bot places a real resting
  `StopOrder` at entry, which legitimately triggers when the market trades through it.

It also matches the bot's **code** (not just the README): the profit ladder is gated by
*remaining* quantity, so the **first contract only comes off at +150%** (3→+150%,
2→+100%, 1→+50%), one contract per poll. Strike = the 3rd listed strike strictly beyond
the breakout price (`floor(px)+3` calls / `ceil(px)−3` puts, $1 spacing).

P&L is **per the 3-contract position, in dollars, with NO commissions or slippage.**

| Best / worst trade | +$1,851 / −$666 | +$1,513 / −$573 |

This is a textbook **low-win-rate, positive-skew option-buyer**: it wins only ~1 in 4
trades, but the average winner is ~3× the average loser. In 2025 that skew was enough to
finish **net positive before costs**; in 2026 (partial) the losers ran larger and it was
**net negative**.

~2 of 3 trades hit the −50% stop. The book is carried entirely by the ~17–19% of trades
that reach the +150% ladder and scale out as premium runs.

## Methodology & Caveats

- **Data.** Underlying = QQQ 1-min RTH bars (repo `qqq_1m_2018_2026.csv.xz`, supplemented
  by monthly backups for Apr–Jun 2026). Options = real 0DTE QQQ 1-min OHLC (Thetadata
  parquet). Entry/exit fills = option **open of the bar after** the signal bar; stop fills
  intrabar at the stop price.
- **No transaction costs.** Commissions and slippage are **not** modelled. At ~$0.65/
  contract/side, a typical trade (3 in, up to 3 out) is ≈$4–6; across ~490 trades that is
  roughly **−$1,900** of drag in 2025 — which turns the +$2,892 gross into a **thin, near-
  breakeven** net. Buying at the ask (vs. the open used here) erodes it further. **2026 is
  clearly net-negative after costs.**
- **Stop fills assume the stop price** (no gap-through slippage), which is mildly optimistic
  for fast-moving 0DTE premium.
- **Re-entry.** After any exit the underlying must trade back inside the opening range before
  a new entry; after a stop-out the 15-minute cooldown also applies. The $1,000 daily-loss
  cap blocks new entries (open positions are still managed).
- **2026 is partial** (through 2026-06-12) and excludes ~12 late-March sessions whose
  underlying source lacked volume (VWAP could not be computed).

## Note on a prior (biased) revision

An earlier version of this backtest contained **look-ahead bias**: it filled profit targets
at the *intrabar high* at the exact target price, and used an ascending 50→100→150% ladder
that does not match the bot's code. Removing the look-ahead (poll-close signals, next-open
fills) **and** fixing the ladder (first scale-out at +150%) changed 2025 from −$433 to
**+$2,892** and 2026 from −$4,121 to **−$3,426**. The corrected figures above supersede it.

## Bottom line

With look-ahead removed and the exit ladder matched to the live code, the ORB + VWAP filter
is a **positive-skew, low-win-rate** strategy that was **modestly profitable in 2025 and
loss-making in 2026 before costs** — and only **marginal-to-negative after realistic
commissions and slippage**. Its edge lives entirely in a handful of trend days; it needs
either lower trade frequency (better entry selectivity) or cheaper execution to be robustly
net-positive. Re-run with commissions/ask-side fills before risking capital.
